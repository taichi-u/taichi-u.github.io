Research & development

2020–2021Wind-based artificial gravity＋

An SSH independent project explored whether airflow could reproduce loading on the body in microgravity. I calculated internal stresses, built an Arduino-based force sensor, and tested a prototype. The analysis suggested that equivalent loading was limited to the soles of the feet; the discrepancy increased higher in the body, identifying a need to improve the approach.

2021.12.19Intelligent Water Rocket Competition＋

As SeSDA representative, I led development of the rocket, onboard electronics, and launcher, as well as communication and budget management. Our missions included parachute deployment, airborne filming, and deployment of an onboard satellite and its parachute. All missions succeeded, earning the Missions Award.

2020.12–2023.03Extreme halophiles at ELSI＋

After discovering astrobiology through online seminars, I contacted researchers and joined Kosuke Fujishima’s lab at Tokyo Tech. With Keio GSC / JST support, I isolated archaea under varied culture conditions and performed DNA and phylogenetic analysis. In one analysis, eight colonies yielded five identified species. The project included rock salt from nine countries and 55 culture conditions.

2021–2022International meteorite research＋

Collaborated with a UK high school and university researchers on meteorite composition and organic material. Used scanning electron microscopy and presented in English at International Research for School.

2023–2024Microbial metabolism＋

Wet-lab research at Kyoto University’s Atomi Lab on microbial pathways for hydrogen and oxygen utilization.

2024.08–Georgia Tech: metagenomic analysis＋

At Thomas DiChristina’s lab, analyzed wetland microbial communities relevant to Savannah River Site remediation. Used Python and R to study the effects of Fe(III), sulfate, and pH buffers. Explored a possible role of organic sulfur shuttles in metal reduction; this was a research hypothesis, not a confirmed mechanism. Continued collaboration remotely after the visit.

2025.02–04DFKI: robot arm control＋

At DFKI Bremen’s Robotics Innovation Center, built Drake simulation environments and controllers for a 7-DOF arm. Considered contact dynamics in the context of spacecraft capture and debris removal. Fully funded through the Nakatani Foundation.
